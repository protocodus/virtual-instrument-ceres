# Shared SUB relative-polarity correction

The independently checked PN3/R7/M routing shows that positive shared SUB raises both filter cutoff and common VCA gain. Ceres previously used opposite signs. The corrected standalone DSP is retained on branch `codex/ceres-sub-polarity-20261018`, commit `d5e0b2d`, in `/tmp/ceres-sub-polarity-20261018`. The Rack release and historical frozen checkout are unchanged.

The production change reverses only the SUB term in the common gain equation. Existing midpoint, depth, clipping and free-running source conventions remain provisional. [The retained diff](candidate.patch) and [source/test evidence](candidate-validation.md) describe the primary-source trace. All seven Release and seven sanitizer checks pass. A direct baseline/candidate comparison at zero tremolo is byte-identical for 384,000 float samples across four rates and eight scenes, including active filter modulation, ring, noise, controllers and reset.

The [recording comparison](recording-comparison.json) keeps every result from two eight-case runs. The six zero-tremolo recordings remain byte-identical. At the same initial clock phase, the two Brass reconstructions reverse their pure-AM phase: harmonic MAE worsens from 3.510/3.514 to 3.626/3.635 dB, and amplitude tails worsen. These failures are retained. A separate research-only half-cycle clock control restores seven baseline WAVs exactly; Brass history20 differs by at most 1.86264515e−9 per sample, with less than 3e−10 dB harmonic-score difference.

No recording phase, gate, gain, envelope or depth was fitted. The half-cycle control is an algebraic check of the inherited pure-AM reconstruction, not a recovered hardware setting or a production reset change. The correction makes the shared modulation relationship agree with the circuit. It does not improve or identify the raw factory filter/envelope mapping, and these reused development clips do not validate simultaneous VCF/VCA modulation against known hardware controls.
