# Tone at 1×, 2× and 4× oversampling, 1.1.7

2026-11-10. Development-container measurements of the engine alone; no host
and no CS-50 recording is involved. The reference is the engine's own 4×
setting, and for the filter its 768 kHz response.

## What was wrong

1× became the default in 1.1.6. Played against 4×, bright patches lost their
top octave. Three sampling effects caused it:

1. The filter's trapezoidal integrators map the whole frequency axis onto
   0…fs/2, so the low-pass slope bends down to a zero at fs/2. With the
   cutoff at 6 kHz and 48 kHz/1×, the response was 5.5 dB under the circuit
   at 12.5 kHz and 12.7 dB under it at 16 kHz.
2. The cutoff stopped at 0.22 fs: 10.6 kHz at 48 kHz/1×. The CS-50's levers
   reach past 20 kHz.
3. The oscillator's edges are band-limited with a two-point polynomial
   residual, which weights every harmonic by sinc²(f/fs): −0.8 dB at 8 kHz,
   −2.0 dB at 12.7 kHz and −3.3 dB at 16 kHz at 48 kHz/1×.

## What changed

- Above 0.22 fs the integrator gain continues as a straight line to 0.88 fs
  instead of stopping.
- The low-pass output is `l − β·h`, with β = 0.69 t² / (1 + 0.63 d² t² +
  0.18 t⁴), t = π·fc/fs and d the stage's damping. Theory gives β ≈ (2/3) t²
  for small t; the three numbers are fitted to the same network's response at
  768 kHz, not derived.
- The saw-and-pulse sum and the sine each pass a minimum-phase three-tap
  filter with magnitude 1 + sin²(πf/fs)/2, which is within 0.25 dB of
  1/sinc² up to 12.7 kHz at 48 kHz and 0.5 dB under it at 16 kHz.
- The wider gain range lets the filter's seven Newton steps fail to close in
  heavily overdriven patches (0.4% of solves in randomised full-drive
  patches). A bracketed solve now finishes those.

`Tests/FilterRateTests.cpp` holds the result and fails on 1.1.6.

Over all counted bands from 4 kHz up, 1× against 1.1.6's 4× went from 4.19 dB
rms (worst 17.9 dB) to 0.68 dB rms (worst 3.3 dB). At a 44.1 kHz host it went
from 5.24 dB rms (worst 21.9 dB) to 0.97 dB rms (worst 5.5 dB, at 16 kHz).

1.1.7 at 1× against 1.1.7 at 4×, 48 kHz: within −0.1…+1.3 dB up to 10 kHz,
−0.8…+0.3 dB at 12.7 kHz and −4.0…−0.4 dB at 16 kHz (median −1.9 dB). At
44.1 kHz: −0.3…+1.4 dB up to 10 kHz, −1.3…0.0 dB at 12.7 kHz and −6.3…−0.7 dB
at 16 kHz (median −3.0 dB).

2× and 4× rise a little because they had the same three effects at their own
rates; 1.1.6 at 4× was about 0.55 dB under the 768 kHz circuit response at
16 kHz.

## The filter alone

1× against the same network at 768 kHz, cutoffs 1, 3 and 6 kHz, RESONANCE 0
and 0.6, 44.1 and 48 kHz hosts:

| | 1.1.6 | 1.1.7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worst error up to 12.5 kHz | 6.19 dB | 0.69 dB |
| Worst error at 16 kHz | 13.18 dB | 1.67 dB |
| 20 kHz cutoff: level at 10 kHz against the passband | −5.9 dB | −2.0 dB |

What remains: a cutoff above 10 kHz at high resonance is up to 2.6 dB high at
12.5 kHz and 6 dB low at 16 kHz, and the high-pass stage's 180 pF pole
(33.4 kHz) still loses 0.4 dB at 12.5 kHz and 1.2 dB at 16 kHz at 1×.
Prewarping that capacitor at 16 kHz removed the loss and put a 3 dB error on
the low-pass resonance peak, so it was not kept.

## Aliasing

The edge correction is a linear filter after the oscillator, so it raises a
harmonic and the alias beside it by the same amount; so does opening the
filter's top. What 1× lost above 10 kHz had been hiding aliasing there. Saw
only, low-pass at 20 kHz, 48 kHz host, energy that is not on a harmonic
against the whole signal:

| Note | Band | 1.1.6 at 1× | 1.1.7 at 1× |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C6 | under 5 kHz | −73.4 dB | −72.8 dB |
| C6 | 5–10 kHz | −59.0 dB | −54.9 dB |
| C6 | over 10 kHz | −54.5 dB | −41.3 dB |
| C7 | over 10 kHz | −51.0 dB | −38.5 dB |

1.1.7 at 2× has −57.8 dB over 10 kHz on C6, and 4× about −78 dB. A
four-point band-limited edge would lower the 1× figures (an offline trial
gave about 27 dB at 5–10 kHz and 6 dB above 10 kHz); it is not in this
version.

## CPU

Instruction counts, `Tools/callgrind_presets.cpp` under Callgrind (GCC 13,
`-O3`), the 14 factory presets at four voices:

| | 1.1.6 | 1.1.7 tone correction | change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1×, 240 blocks | 1,267,286,139 | 1,329,370,594 | +4.9% |
| 4×, 60 blocks | 1,274,041,748 | 1,335,670,856 | +4.8% |

All of it is in the per-sample path: the two edge-level taps per board, and
per filter the low-pass correction's coefficient (one division whenever the
cutoff moves) and its term at the output. `RenderCeresPresetDemos --cpu`
reads 0.017–0.041× real time for the demos, with the worst block at 5.0% of
one core. Development-container figures, not a Reason or DAW result.
